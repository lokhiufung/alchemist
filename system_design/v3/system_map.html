System map · Goal

Goal

Execute a strategy book at the broker across asset classes (futures and stocks in v3; options in a later version, decision 54), instruments, exchanges and currencies, placing the same orders whether it runs on a simulated broker, a paper account or a live account.

Not this system's job

  • Strategy research: finding signals, fitting params, or any tooling that produces them.
  • Comparing the engine's trades with research results, and monitoring strategies for decay.
  • Keeping a registry of strategies and their params versions.
  • Version control of strategies and their params: the strategy code under local/strategies/ belongs to its author.
  • Portfolio reporting in a base currency (see decision 39).

System map · Introduction

Introduction

Alchemist v3 is an execution engine for strategies on futures and stocks (options in a later version). Its inputs are the broker's market data (or a replay source standing in for the broker), daily auxiliary series from outside data sources, plus deployment config supplied from outside and the author's strategy classes, which hold each signal's params. Its first book trades CME equity-index futures in USD and Eurex DAX futures in EUR. Its only trading consumer is the broker, which receives its orders. A second outside actor, the operator, sends deploy requests and receives status lines and alerts. Secondary outputs are logs, bar and trade recordings, and, in replay, the broker journal (decision 2).

  1. Market data enters through the Gateway.
  2. The Event Loop turns it into bars and dispatches them. Before dispatch, Aux Data adds daily auxiliary values from outside data sources to each bar, and tells each strategy that reads them whether it is ready.
  3. Each Strategy steps its StrategySignal and passes the bar to its StrategyExecution state machine, which asks the signal whether to enter or exit.
  4. The resulting orders are tracked by the Order Manager and leave again through the Gateway.
  5. Alongside, Safety watches the broker connection, reconnects it, and repairs any strategy left waiting on an order during an outage.

Every instrument names its exchange profile and currency, so every bar, order, fill and position knows where it trades and what money it is counted in.

System map · System boundary

System boundary

For each component: what it owns, what it leaves to others, what it receives, and what it produces.

Gateway

OwnsAll communication with the broker: market data in, orders and cancels out, fills and executions back, for every instrument the process trades. A replay source can stand in for the broker behind it.
Not its jobBuilding bars or deciding who hears an event (Event Loop); deciding what to trade (Strategy, StrategyExecution); tracking an order's life (Order Manager); noticing a dead connection or repairing strategies after one (Safety).
ReceivesFrom the broker: market data and fills. From Order Manager: orders and cancels, each with its order id. From Safety: heartbeat checks, reconnect and resubscribe requests, requests for executions, open orders and positions. From the Event Loop: history requests for warmup; contract, subscribe and unsubscribe requests; session declarations.
ProducesTo the Event Loop: tick_update and bar_update messages (each gateway declares which one it gives per instrument, decision 49), order, position and balance updates, and warmup history. To Order Manager: the next order id. To the broker: orders and cancels. To Safety: connection health, the last tick time and the broker's executions, open orders and positions.

Event Loop

OwnsTurning tick_update messages into completed bars, taking bar_update messages as completed bars, and delivering bars and order updates to the right strategies, in time order, exactly once; and seeding each strategy with the warmup history it needs (daily and prior-session), fetched through Gateway and retried until it lands.
Not its jobThe broker connection, and building daily bars from intraday history (Gateway); entry/exit decisions (Strategy, StrategyExecution); recovering from an outage (Safety).
ReceivesFrom Gateway: tick_update and bar_update messages, order, position and balance updates, and warmup history. From Aux Data: values on each bar. From Safety: repaired fill and Rejected events, position updates, and a check-in request.
ProducesTo Strategy: completed bars, order, position and balance updates, warmup history, flatten requests and quarantine calls. To Gateway: history requests. To Safety: check-ins and read access to the runtime book's members. To disk: bar and trade recordings (paper and live).
Runtime bookAlso owns the runtime book, the one registry of the strategies the process runs and the per-instrument resources serving them, and adding one strategy to it while the process runs (decisions 34–36). Receives, from the runner's deploy socket in paper/live (decision 51): deploy requests as book operations. Produces, to the runner: one status per step until a terminal status, which the runner streams to the operator. Produces, to Gateway: contract, subscribe and unsubscribe requests for a new strategy's instruments.

Aux Data (decision 44)

OwnsReading auxiliary daily series from outside data sources, each through one data-source interface; deciding when a value is settled for its target session; arming and disarming each group of strategies that read them; adding the values to bars; and serving the history frames a cold rebuild needs.
Not its jobBroker history (Gateway, requested by the Event Loop); delivering bars (Event Loop); when a signal may be rebuilt and telling StrategyExecution it is ready (Strategy); how a calculation is seeded (StrategySignal).
ReceivesFrom outside data sources (files in replay): auxiliary daily series. From the Event Loop: each completed bar before dispatch, and the runtime book's members. From Strategy: frame requests on a cold rebuild.
ProducesTo the Event Loop: bars with auxiliary values added. To Strategy: ready / not ready, with the history frames.

Strategy

OwnsBeing the one per-slot object the Event Loop talks to: booking its own position from fills, in the instrument's currency, and driving its StrategySignal, StrategyExecution and Order Manager with what the Event Loop delivers — stepping the signal on each bar, passing the bar on to StrategyExecution, and deciding when the signal may be rebuilt (only while flat with no open or submitted orders).
Not its jobIts params (StrategySignal holds them); the calculations and entry/exit logic (StrategySignal); which execution phase the slot is in or what an order event means in it (StrategyExecution); an order's life from submission to closed status (Order Manager); when bars are complete (Event Loop).
ReceivesFrom the Event Loop: completed bars, order updates and warmup history. From Aux Data: ready / not ready and history frames. From outside: deployment config. From StrategySignal: warm/not-warm. From StrategyExecution: whether it is processing an order. From Order Manager: ownership answers.
ProducesTo StrategySignal: bars with position data (entry price, seconds since entry) and rebuild requests. To StrategyExecution: bars and order updates. To Order Manager: order updates and ownership checks.

StrategySignal

OwnsThe slot's signal: its calculation DAG and params, stepping the calculations on each bar in dependency order, evaluating the entry and exit logic, reporting whether it is warm, and how to rebuild itself cold by replaying seed bars. Written as a Python SignalDag subclass that holds its own params (decision 42).
Not its jobWhen it is stepped or rebuilt (Strategy); the position or fills beyond what Strategy passes in (Strategy); acting on its signals, or when its exit inputs are frozen and released (StrategyExecution); orders (Order Manager); talking to the Event Loop (Strategy).
ReceivesFrom Strategy: bars with position data, and rebuild requests. From StrategyExecution: entry/exit questions and exit-input freeze/release.
ProducesTo StrategyExecution: entry/exit signals. To Strategy: warm/not-warm.

StrategyExecution

OwnsThe slot's execution state machine: which phase it is in, when to enter and exit by asking the slot's signal, and what each order event means in the current phase.
Not its jobThe signal's logic or when it is stepped (StrategySignal, Strategy); the position (Strategy); an order's life at the broker (Order Manager); talking to the Event Loop (Strategy).
ReceivesFrom Strategy: bars and order updates. From StrategySignal: entry/exit signals. From Order Manager: answers about its orders (status and filled quantity, submitted and open orders, group finality).
ProducesTo Order Manager: entry and exit orders, cancels, and order queries. To StrategySignal: entry/exit questions and exit-input freeze/release. To Strategy: its phase, through query answers.

Order Manager

OwnsThe life of every order the slot sends: building it, tracking it from submission to a closed status, knowing which orders the slot owns, and sending cancels.
Not its jobDeciding to enter or exit (StrategyExecution); the position (Strategy); broker communication (Gateway).
ReceivesFrom StrategyExecution: entry and exit orders, cancels, and order queries. From Strategy: order updates and ownership checks.
ProducesTo Gateway: orders and cancels. To Strategy: ownership answers. To StrategyExecution: query answers (its submitted and open orders, group finality, filled quantities).

Safety

OwnsDetecting a lost broker connection or a frozen process, reconnecting, un-sticking any strategy left waiting on an order whose fill happened during the outage, and the periodic stuck-order check (decision 50).
Not its jobTalking to the broker itself (Gateway); deciding what to trade (Strategy, StrategyExecution); normal event delivery (Event Loop).
ReceivesFrom Gateway: connection health, the last tick time, and the broker's executions, open orders and positions. From the Event Loop: check-ins and the runtime book's members. From Strategy: each slot's submitted, open and cancelling orders, and "processing an order?".
ProducesTo Gateway: heartbeat checks, reconnect and resubscribe requests, and queries. To the Event Loop: repaired fill and Rejected events and position updates, through the event buffer. To the operator: CRITICAL logs and stack dumps.

Shared, owned by no component

  • Exchange profiles — one per exchange: sessions, calendar, venue, currency, contract identity and, for futures, roll (decision 31).
  • Instruments — asset class, symbol, exchange profile, currency, multiplier, tick size (decision 37, 41).
  • Configuration loading and structured logging.
  • Per decision 46: Clock — now() and wait(timeout), as WallClock or ReplayClock; and RunMode — the run mode, built once by the runner. The Event Loop moves the ReplayClock forward to each bar's end time in emit_bar; in replay wait(timeout) returns at once.
  • Per decision 51: Runner — run_strategies.py, Runtime.build and the deploy socket server. The runner builds the components in this order: Clock and RunMode, Gateway, Event Loop and runtime book, Aux Data, the slots (Strategy with its StrategySignal, StrategyExecution and Order Manager), Safety. The runner stops them in reverse. The runner owns the local deploy socket and hands each deploy request to the Event Loop as a book operation.

Golden sample · frozen

Agreed 2026-11-09. This sample replaces the earlier L-cross sample (a fixed rule on a few minutes of hand-made prices) (decisions 11, 12, 48).

The golden sample is one replay run of the real deployment path. The run uses the real entry point, a real deployment config and a real strategy. Only the broker is different: the simulated broker replaces IBKR (decision 6). The run is deterministic: the same input files give the same output bytes on every machine. Thus every pass and fail condition is an exact value.

Per component

ComponentExpected behaviour
GatewayThe simulated broker answers daily history and seed bars from the 60 prior sessions, delivers all 23,400 replay bars as bar_update messages in time order, then end of data. Each MKT order fills at the close of the bar that made the decision. The simulated broker accepts exactly two orders, returns one fill for each, and writes four journal rows.
Event LoopSeeds the slot before the first replay bar. Delivers each of the 23,400 bars to the strategy exactly once, in time order. Delivers both fills. Stops after end of data with the book flat.
StrategySets aux_ready when the slot is built, because the signal reads no auxiliary name. Steps the signal on every bar. Books long 1 MES on the buy fill and flat on the sell fill. Ends flat.
StrategySignalIs warm on the first replay bar, with daily ATR = 20.00. Entry is false in phase A and true on one bar in phase B. Exit target is the first exit that is true after the entry.
StrategyExecutionStays flat in phase A. Decides one buy in phase B. Moves to in-position on the buy fill. Decides one sell when the target is reached. Returns to flat on the sell fill. Decides nothing in phase C.
Order ManagerSubmits exactly two market orders, tracks each one to filled, and ends with no open or submitted order.
SafetyReplay uses the do-nothing stand-in (decision 46). Nothing to check in the golden run; Safety is proved by its own component tests.
Aux DataThe strategy reads no auxiliary name: Aux Data reads no source, arms nothing and adds nothing to any bar.

End to end

The broker journal is the output that the test reads. The simulated broker writes one JSON line for each order that the simulated broker receives (time, order id, side, quantity, order type, instrument) and one JSON line for each fill that the simulated broker sends (time, order id, price, quantity).

Journal ruleValue
TimeReplayClock time in UTC, ISO-8601 with milliseconds. A fill row has the end time of the bar it filled on.
KeysSorted by name; no spaces after separators.
PricesWritten with 2 decimals.
Order idsThe simulated broker numbers orders from 1 in each run.
LabelValue
InputThe entry point, config, frozen strategy copy and synthetic tape above.
ExpectedOne BUY 1 MES MKT in phase B and its fill; one SELL 1 MES MKT at the target and its fill; the book ends flat.
Passes whenAll of these checks are true:
  1. The process exit code is 0.
  2. The sha256 of the bar_update stream that the simulated broker sends equals expected/tape.sha256.
  3. The broker journal equals expected/broker_journal.jsonl byte for byte.
  4. The journal has exactly 4 rows: BUY order, BUY fill, SELL order, SELL fill, in this order. Each order is 1 MES, type MKT.
  5. The BUY order time is at or after 09:40:00 (phase B) and before 15:30:00 (last_entry_bars = 1800).
  6. SELL fill price − BUY fill price ≥ 10.00. Thus the realised profit is at least 10.00 × 5 USD = 50.00 USD.
  7. A second run with the same input gives a byte-identical broker journal.
  8. The log has 0 ERROR lines and 0 CRITICAL lines.
Fails whenOne or more of the checks is false. The test report names each false check; for check 3 it shows the first journal row that is different.

Checks 4 to 6 come from the designed price path, not from the frozen journal. Thus a wrong journal cannot become the expected answer by a careless refresh. The frozen journal and the tape fingerprint change only in a commit that says why they changed.

Decisions already made

Goal and boundary

1What is the system's one goal?

Execute a strategy book at the broker, placing the same orders on a simulated broker, a paper account or a live account.

2Who consumes the output?

The broker is the only trading consumer: only the broker receives orders. The operator is a second outside actor: the operator sends deploy requests and receives status lines, alerts and CRITICAL logs. Secondary outputs, read by people and outside tools: logs in a stable format (decision 29), bar and trade recordings, and in replay the broker journal.

changed 2026-11-09 · was "the broker only"

3Are strategy research, comparing results with research, a strategy/params registry or decay monitoring inside the boundary?

None of them. None of that work happens here, so they are left out of the map.

4How many components?

Eight: Gateway, Event Loop, Aux Data, Strategy, StrategySignal, StrategyExecution, Order Manager, Safety.

Rejected: folding Safety into Gateway (it fails and recovers independently of trading); a separate Warmup component (not needed at this level).

changed 2026-11-09: eight, with Aux Data (decision 44)

5Where do strategy params and deployment config sit?

Deployment config is an external input, not a component. Strategy params sit inside each strategy's signal class (decision 42): they are code the strategy author writes, not config.

Rejected: a Configuration component (the engine reads config, it does not own it); params in outside config (a second place that must agree with the code).

changed 2026-11-07 · params were an external input

6How do simulation/replay modes relate to the boundary?

A swap behind the Gateway: a simulated broker that replays data and fills orders sits behind the same interface as the real one, so every other component runs unchanged.

Rejected: a separate Simulator component, a simulation path that feeds strategies directly, and leaving sim out.

7The broker is both source and sink — one Gateway, or split feed/orders?

Keep one Gateway per broker, serving every instrument the process trades there. The diagram's order arrow is labelled as leaving through the Gateway.

Rejected: a Market Feed / Order Router split.

8What does Safety exchange?

Heartbeat and reconnect with Gateway, executions from Gateway, repaired fill events into the Event Loop.

Rejected: detection-only.

A normal input through the Gateway on the normal path.

Splitting the slot

14Why split StrategyExecution and Order Manager out of Strategy?

Separation of work: Order Manager handles the order lifecycle, StrategyExecution handles the execution state machine, and Strategy is the higher-level object that uses both to interact with the Event Loop and consume its updates.

Resting broker-side exits (OCA groups) made both concerns the hard part.

15Who talks to Order Manager?

Both: StrategyExecution sends entry and exit orders and cancels and queries its own orders; Strategy forwards order updates and checks ownership.

Rejected: routing everything through StrategyExecution, and Strategy relaying Order Manager's answers to StrategyExecution in an order-context object.

16Who owns the position?

Strategy.

Rejected: moving it into Order Manager.

17Does the golden sample change with the split?

No: the same bar-close round trip, with expected behaviour for StrategyExecution and Order Manager.

Rejected: a resting-exit sample.

18Does the system's goal or external boundary change with the split?

No; only the internal split changes.

19Why split StrategySignal out of Strategy?

It is deterministic (bars and position data in, entry/exit answers out, no I/O), and it is where most warmup and signal bugs live; giving it its own boundary lets it be tested alone, from bars in to answers out. Strategy becomes the driver of StrategySignal, StrategyExecution and Order Manager, and stays the only component the Event Loop talks to.

20Who calls the signal?

Strategy and StrategyExecution: Strategy steps StrategySignal on each bar and rebuilds it; StrategyExecution asks it for the entry or exit answer in the phase that needs one. The Event Loop never reads the signal; it goes through Strategy.

Rejected: Strategy evaluating both answers and handing them to StrategyExecution — evaluating exit while flat, or entry before warmup, reads calculations that do not exist yet, and choosing by phase would leak the state machine into Strategy.

21Does the signal see more than bars?

Yes, position data: Strategy passes strategy data (entry price, seconds since entry) into each step, and StrategyExecution times the exit-input freeze (entry-decision bar) and release (flat again). The signal never reads Strategy's position itself. It answers exit per condition as well as overall, so StrategyExecution can mask conditions a resting broker order already covers.

22Who owns the signal rebuild (cold reset + seed replay)?

Split: StrategySignal owns how; Strategy owns when — only while flat with no open or submitted orders, which needs position and Order Manager state the signal must not see.

23Who seeds strategies with warmup history (fetch, retry, auxiliary data)?

The Event Loop. It already holds the per-instrument plumbing and the loop the retries run on; Strategy decides only when a rebuild may happen (decision 22) and the signal how. Still no separate Warmup component (decision 4).

Latency and concurrency (R3)

25What latency does R3 measure?

Replaced by decision 55: feed latency and processing latency; the 5 ms budget applies to processing latency. The 5 ms budget is end-to-end latency: from the broker timestamp of the tick that closes a bar to that bar's order leaving the Gateway for the broker, at p99. It is logged per minute as p50/p99 together with its two internal parts: slot cost (one strategy, one bar) and dispatch latency (a bar's closing event entering the event buffer → the last strategy on that instrument done). Because feed transit counts against the budget, the engine's own share is what is left after transit.

Rejected: bar age measured from the bar's nominal end time — it includes the wait for the next tick that closes the bar (often hundreds of ms in a quiet market), which no engine design can remove. Rejected: counting only internal processing (slot cost or dispatch latency alone) — the budget is from the broker's time to the order.

agreed 2026-11-09

26How does the Event Loop learn there is work?

A drain wake-up: every producer of the event buffer raises it after appending (the broker's reader thread through a thread-safe call into the loop), and the loop waits on it with a timeout equal to the bar-flush cadence (20 ms), so the clock-driven bar flush still runs on time. The event buffer stays a deque. This removes a 0–20 ms queue wait on every event that a fixed polling timer would add. A producer raises the wake-up only when none is pending, the buffer drains a control lane before a data lane, and each pass has a batch budget (Event Loop decision 8).

Rejected: a shorter timer (more idle wake-ups and still a floor); an asyncio queue everywhere (replay preloads the whole day into the buffer and puts fills at its front).

agreed 2026-11-09

27What happens when the event buffer backs up?

It stays lossless (order updates are never dropped), but a control-lane backlog over a threshold becomes an ERROR with an operator alert, and the size of each lane and the queue wait are logged with each drain pass. The data lane has no alert.

Rejected: a hard size limit that drops events — it could drop a fill.

agreed 2026-11-09

28What concurrency model runs the strategies?

One process, one asyncio event loop, the broker's reader thread and one send thread; strategies on a bar are stepped one after another, in time order. One process keeps dispatch latency under 5 ms at p99 up to about 10 strategies active at once; R3's budget is on processing latency (decision 55), which adds queue wait to dispatch latency, so that count is an upper bound to be re-measured. The load target is 3 tick-by-tick streams and 10–15 strategies; whether one process carries it is decided by a measured scale trigger, not by a strategy count (Event Loop decision 9). Beyond that, split into process shards by strategy group, with one process still owning the single Gateway (decision 7 holds). Before sharding, Safety and its repair need a design that covers every shard.

Rejected: a thread per strategy (the GIL serialises CPU-bound Python anyway); an asyncio task per strategy (no parallelism, and the time-order guarantee gets harder to keep); one actor per strategy and gateway with message ports between them (no global time order, and ports wired by hand); sharding before it is needed.

agreed 2026-11-09

29Where are per-bar logs written?

Off the loop thread: loggers hand records to a queue and a listener thread writes the files. Record content and field names are a stable format that outside tools may read.

Rejected: dropping per-bar indicator logs to DEBUG — they are how a run is audited afterwards.

agreed 2026-11-09

33How is slot cost brought down before sharding?

Profile one strategy's bar first: logging is a small part of slot cost, so the rest is signal stepping and the execution state machine. Then consider computing a calculation once for all strategies on the same instrument when its registry name and params are identical. That would change who owns a calculation's state, so it needs its own decision first. This reduces sharding but does not replace it.

Extensibility (R2)

30What is the Gateway interface?

An abstract interface. Required methods have no body, so a gateway that misses one fails when it is built instead of returning nothing. Optional methods are gateway capabilities declared on the interface with safe defaults, never looked up by name at runtime. Gateways are picked by name from one fixed gateway table in the engine, GATEWAYS, because users do not write their own gateways. History is requested in broker-neutral units (months or dates), not broker-specific duration strings. See Gateway.

Rejected: splitting the Gateway (decision 7 holds); a decorator registry for gateways (dropped 2026-11-10: only built-in gateways exist, so a fixed table is enough).

Every component reads it; no other file branches on an exchange name. CME, Eurex and NASDAQ (decision 54) are the first profiles.

Rejected: one component owning the calendar — the Event Loop, Gateway, Safety and StrategySignal all read it; special cases for one exchange spread over the files that need them.

Add a strategy without a restart (R5)

34Who owns the set of strategies the process runs?

The Event Loop, as one runtime book: strategies in dispatch order, per-instrument resamplers and bar buffers, contracts, the recording session maps and the background retry tasks. Only the drain-loop task changes it, and only at a commit boundary: another task hands it a book operation and awaits the result, so a bar or order dispatch already in flight never sees the book change. Everyone else reads membership from the book at call time — shutdown, Safety's repair and positions poll, the auxiliary retry, the recorders and the reconnect resubscribe — so a strategy added later is a full member. Cold start and hot-add go through the same steps: reserve instruments, stage, prepare, commit.

Rejected: each consumer keeping its own copy of the strategy list made at start; a book changed from any task (a dispatch could see a half-added strategy).

agreed 2026-11-09

35How does an operator add a strategy to a running process?

A separate client script, deploy_strategy.py --config FILE --key KEY. It reads the same config file to find the socket and the sha256, sends one request and exits with the terminal status. The runner, in paper and live only, listens on a local socket next to its logs (directory 0700, socket 0600). One deploy request per connection names one strategy key and the config file's sha256; the runner streams status lines (preparing, then one of registered, noop, rejected, failed, busy, shutting_down) with a reason and the revision (the config file's sha256). Which version of a strategy's code runs is not tracked: version control of strategies is outside the system. Requests run one at a time. Every terminal status is logged with the same hash fields as the startup deploy fingerprint. The slot is first added to the config file, so the next cold start runs it too: the deploy request is the existing deployment-config input arriving while the process runs (decision 5 holds). Simulation never opens the socket.

Rejected: a --deploy flag on the runner script (a short socket client and the long-running driver would share one parser and one main); a second service; the runner polling the config file.

36What may a deploy request do?

Add one new strategy key, nothing else, and only when it cannot disturb what is running. It is refused, before anything is touched, when the config file changed after the request was made (stale_config), the key is not in the config (unknown_key), the strategy folder fails to import (import_error), the signal's params fail their type check (params_invalid), the config differs anywhere but the new slot (config_drift), the key already runs (conflict), the strategy's session is open (session_open), its next session closes after this process exits (after_process_exit), no running strategy uses its account (unknown_account_route), or a position or order on its execution instrument belongs to no running strategy, compared per account (unmanaged_exposure; nothing is cancelled or flattened). A failure after that cleans only what the candidate created; shared feeds and running strategies are never touched. A committed strategy starts gated and can first enter at its next session open, once warmup and auxiliary data are ready.

Rejected as out of scope: changing or removing a running strategy, reloading code or params, starting a strategy inside its own session, adding several strategies atomically, new account routes, changing when the process exits, automatic recovery from quarantine.

Multi-asset and multi-currency (R1)

37What is an instrument?

StrategySignal model

42How is a StrategySignal written?

As a Python SignalDag subclass, registered by name. It declares its params as a typed, frozen dataclass whose defaults are the values, and builds its DAG in build_graph() from registered calculations, inline Python expressions, named conditions and named exits. Custom strategies

43How does the engine load a custom strategy?

run_strategies.py is the only entry point that runs strategies. At start it imports every folder under local/strategies/ by file path: calculations.py, strategy_signal.py and strategy.py. Open questions decided 2026-11-09

52Which readiness state gates trading?

One gate: StrategyExecution's ready_to_arm = warmup_ready AND aux_ready. Each flag has one writer, through Strategy. warmup_ready is set when the Event Loop's seed state is READY and the signal reports warm. aux_ready is set when Aux Data arms the slot's group, or at build when the signal reads no auxiliary name. The Event Loop's seed state (PENDING, READY, QUARANTINED_*) only drives the seed retry; Aux Data's arming only drives aux_ready. Neither one is read by StrategyExecution directly.

Rejected: StrategyExecution reading the seed state or the arm groups (a reach across two boundaries); one combined flag (a late aux value and a late warmup fetch are retried by different components).

decided 2026-11-09

53What happens to late warmup or aux data while a position is open?

Strategy keeps it as a pending rebuild and clears the matching ready flag at once, so the slot makes no new entry. The open trade keeps running on the current signal, and its exits stay live. When the slot is flat and idle, Strategy rebuilds the signal from the pending data and sets the flag again. A newer late value replaces an older pending one. At session end, a pending rebuild that never ran is logged at WARNING.

Rejected: dropping the late data (the next entry would trade on a stale signal); rebuilding inside the trade (the exit logic would change under an open position, Strategy feature 3).

55Which latencies does the engine measure, and which one has the 5 ms budget?

Two measures, both logged per minute as p50 and p99, per instrument.

  1. Feed latency — from the broker's timestamp on a message to the message's arrival in the engine. The Gateway's reader thread stamps recv_ts when each message arrives. Feed latency = recv_ts − broker timestamp. It is measured, not budgeted: the engine does not control it, and its precision is the broker's timestamp precision (IBKR stamps ticks to the second), so the log records that precision with the number.
  2. Processing latency — from a message's arrival (recv_ts) to each strategy's final decision on it: an order or cancel handed to the Gateway, or no action. A bar that the flush timer closes starts at the time the Event Loop closes it. The 5 ms p99 budget of R3 applies to processing latency. Its internal parts, queue wait, dispatch latency and slot cost, are logged with it.

Rejected: one end-to-end number from the broker timestamp (it mixes a part the engine does not control with a part it does, and broker clock offset and second-level timestamps make it unmeasurable at 5 ms); ending at "order leaving the Gateway" only (bars with no order would go unmeasured).
